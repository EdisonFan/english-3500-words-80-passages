# Passage 30

Live with roommates? Have friends and family around you? Chances are that if you're looking to live a more sustainable lifestyle, not everyone around you will be ready to jump on that bandwagon. There may be times when you feel the need to apologize for pushing your ideals onto other family members.

I experienced this when I started switching to a zero waste lifestyle five years ago, as I was living with my parents, and I continue to experience this with my husband, as he refuses to be completely zero waste like me. I've learned a few things along the way though, which I hope you'll find encouraging if you're doing your best to figure out how you can make the change in a not-always-supportive household.

Zero waste was a radical lifestyle movement a few years back. I learned that the stuff we accumulate daily, from plastic packaging to single-use items, contributes significantly to environmental problems. I remember showing my parents a video of Bea Johnson, sharing how cool I thought it would be to buy groceries with jars, and have so little trash! A few days later, I came back with my first jars of zero waste groceries, and my dad commented on how silly it was for me to carry jars everywhere. It came off as a bit discouraging, yet reasonable, considering he was a fan of packaged beer.

Yet as the months of reducing waste continued, I did what I could that was within my own reach. I had my own bedroom, so I worked on removing things I didn't need. Since I had my own toiletries in the bathroom, I was able to switch to bamboo toothbrushes and bar soaps. I replaced single-use items with reusable ones made of cloth or leather, and kept a bin to contain things that can be recycled. I also offered to cook every so often, so I portioned out a bit of the cupboard for my own zero waste groceries. Perhaps your family would still buy milk in plastic bottles or pre-packaged biscuits, but you may have some control over your own personal spaces to make the changes.

If you feel desperate to avoid waste, remember that change takes time. As you make your lifestyle changes, you may find yourself wanting to speak up for yourself if others comment on what you're doing, which can turn itself into a whole household debate. If you have individuals in your family who are not on board, your words probably won't do much. Sometimes it feels like talking to a brick wall and can often leave you feeling more discouraged.
