# Passage 67

Music has long been considered to be an enjoyable pastime for many people. Attending a concert or a music festival brings us wonderful experiences. It is also highly popular due to the individualized effects on relieving stress and anxiety—and its mental health benefits can't be argued. Musicians create healing sounds with their instruments. For example, the quiet sounds of a piano or violin create a peaceful atmosphere that calms the mind.

Some experts say that music can be harmful if it is too noisy. However, at moderate volumes, music can be very beneficial if one is in pain. When we feel anxious, listening to our favorite tunes can bring a sense of harmony to our minds. This, of course, can lead to less pain. Even after surgery, patients often recover faster when they listen to soothing melodies (旋律).

Many people enjoy relaxing music in the evening prior to going to bed. They say that listening to gentle music before bedtime can play a big role in calming the brain enough to sleep, helping them fall asleep more easily. While the validity of the idea is still being assessed, the lowered stress can even be tied back to blood pressure. Similarly, according to researchers, listening to just thirty minutes of soft music every day may help with healthy blood sugar levels, through the lowering of stress and anxiety.

When it comes to heart health, there is speculation (推测) that it's not the style of music, but rather the tempo that makes it so good for your heart health. In one European study, researchers used music at different speeds to stimulate the participants and monitored their heart rates and blood pressure. Interestingly, the more cheerful the music was, the faster their heart rates were. On the other hand, when the music slowed, the participants adjusted to a calmer state, and the effects on heart rates appeared to follow suit.

The implications of music on overall well-being are incredible. It can touch our soul and lift our spirit. But there is a whole range of other health issues for which turning up the radio could be beneficial, which is what makes music so valuable.
