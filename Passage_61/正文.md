# Passage 61

Indoor plants might look as if they just sit around not doing much, but in many ways they are the unsung heroes of the home. Not only do they look beautiful, but studies have shown that they can promote people's well-being. What's more, indoor plants are easy to look after and are not very expensive.

What are indoor plants?

Indoor plants are also known as houseplants or pot plants. A key criterion for indoor plants is that they like to grow indoors. Many of these species are not ideally suited to growing outside in the UK, especially in the winter. On the contrary, they grow better inside, where it is warmer.

Why are indoor plants good for you?

Will Spoelstra, who works at the Royal Botanic Gardens, says, "There are many benefits to growing plants indoors. During the winter months, I've found that having plants around the house can really lift your mood." A pretty plant, when placed in the right spot, can transform an otherwise plain space into a splendid living area. Tending to plants can offer you a temporary escape from the busy daily life. Research has also shown that pot plants can clean the surrounding air by absorbing harmful gases like carbon dioxide. They also remove some harmful chemicals and act as a natural barrier to external noises. Plants like peace lilies and devil's tongue are among the best in these regards.

Which plants can you grow?

Aloe vera, peace lilies, snake plants and spider plants are some of the outstanding plants that are easy to grow indoors. Thus, they are perfect for those who may hesitate to care for delicate plants. The aloe vera has smooth, acid leaves. Inside these leaves is a gel that can treat skin irritation (炎症). The peace lily is well-suited to damp environments and, with its small size, fits perfectly into tight apartment rooms. The snake plant hardly requires much attention, although it gives off a sour scent, which could be a minor drawback. The spider plant has long, striped (有条纹的) leaves that create a vivid contrast in an office space. You can buy plants from supermarkets, garden centers or online. Younger plants are often cheaper than fully grown ones. If you decide to own plants by sowing seeds, you ought to carefully care for them as they mature, making sure they don't "starve" from a lack of water or the sunlight they need.
