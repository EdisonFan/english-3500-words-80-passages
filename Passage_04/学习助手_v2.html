01

"I didn't like the ending,"I said to my favorite college professor.It was my junioryear of undergraduate study, and I was doing an independentstudy on Victorian literature.I had just finished reading <i>The Mill on the Floss</i> by George Eliot, and I was heartbrokenby the ending.Prof.Gracie, with all his patience, asked me to thinkabout it beyond whether I likedit or not.He suggested I think about the differencebetween endings that I wanted for the charactersand endings that were right for the characters, endings that satisfied the story even if they didn't have a traditionallypositive outcome.Of course, I would have favoreda differentending for Tom and Maggie Tulliver, but the ending they got did make the most sensefor them.

02

This was an ahamoment for me, and I never thought about endings the sameway again.From then on, if I wanted to read an ending guaranteed to be happy, I'd pickup a romance novel .If I wanted an ending I couldn't guess, I'd pick up a mystery.For an ending where I kindof knew what was going to happen, I'd choose historical fiction .Choosing what to read becameeasier.

03

But writingendings—that's hard.It's hard for writers because endings carryso much weightwith readers.You have to balance creating an ending that's unpredictable, yet doesn't seem to come from nowhere, one that fits what's right for the characters.

04

That's why this issueof <i>Writers Digest</i> aims to help you figureout how to write the best ending for whatever kind of writing you're doing.If it's short stories, Peter Mountford breaksdown six techniques you can try to see which one helps you stickthe landing.Elizabeth Sims analyzes the final chapters of five greatnovels to see what key points they include and how you can adaptthem for your work.

05

This issue won't tell you what your ending should be—that's upto you and the story you're telling—but it might providewhat you need to get there.

中文译文

PARAGRAPH 01

我对我最喜欢的大学教授说："我不喜欢这个结局。"那是我本科三年级的时候，我正在做一项关于维多利亚文学的独立研究。我刚刚读完乔治·艾略特的《弗洛斯河上的磨坊》，结局令我心碎。格雷西教授非常有耐心，让我不要仅从自己喜欢与否的角度出发，而要深入思考这个问题。他建议我思考一下，我想要的人物结局与适合这些人物的结局之间有什么不同，想想即使没有传统意义上的好结果，也能让故事完整自洽的结局。当然，我更希望汤姆和玛吉·塔利弗有个不一样的结局，但书中的结局对他们来说确实是最合理的。

PARAGRAPH 02

那一刻，我恍然大悟，之后再也没有以同样的方式看待过结局。从那以后，如果我想读一个肯定会幸福的结局，我就会选择爱情小说。如果我想读一个无法预测的结局，那我会选一本悬疑小说。如果是想读一个我大致知道会发生什么的结局，我会选择历史小说。选择阅读内容变得更容易了。

PARAGRAPH 03

但是，要写出结局——那可是很难的。对作家来说很不容易，因为结局对读者来说承载了很重的分量。你必须做到平衡，创作出一个既出人意料又非无中生有的结局，一个符合人物设定的结局。

PARAGRAPH 04

这就是为什么本期《作家文摘》旨在帮助你弄清楚，无论你正在进行何种类型的写作，该如何写出最好的结局。如果是短篇小说，彼得·蒙福德为你分解出了六种技巧，你可以尝试看看哪一种能帮助你顺利完成写作。伊丽莎白·西姆斯分析了五部优秀小说的最终章，阐述了它们包含的关键点以及你如何将这些关键点应用到自己的作品中。

PARAGRAPH 05

本期内容不会告诉你结局应该是什么——这取决于你和你所讲述的故事——但它可能会为你提供通往那个结局所需要的东西。
