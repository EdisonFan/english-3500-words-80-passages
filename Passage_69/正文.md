# Passage 69

As an artist who shares her journey on social media, I'm often asked by curious followers how to begin an art journey. Unfortunately, there is no magic list I can offer. I do remember, though, what it was like to be a complete beginner. It can feel both amazing and awkward. At first, you might feel that any real skill is absent, and you may feel bitter about not reaching your goal as quickly as you'd hoped. So I've put together some good tips for starting an art journey.

Start small. I suggest using a sketchbook ( 素描本) for small studies. These small studies provide inspiration and may be a springboard for more complex works in the future. Don't throw away your amateur art. It's important to acknowledge that art is a long journey. You'll want to look back on your journey to see how far you've come.

Paint often and paint from life. There's no better way to improve than to put in those brush miles. Whether you paint still life, portraits, or landscapes, paint from life as much as possible even when you get bored. By capturing the details of a flower or a fruit, you will develop a better understanding of light and shadow. The painting muscle memory only comes with repetition.

Continually challenge yourself to try something new. Get out of your comfort zone. Artistic growth can be a bit painful. You may doubt how it will turn out. Welcome to the club; we've all been there. Many artists find that their ambition grows once they experiment with different materials or tools—this is often how the most extraordinary works of art are born. Anybody willing to learn can succeed in attaining new levels of expression. When you anticipate challenges, you may find that the results are more rewarding than you expected.

Share your work if you feel comfortable doing so. It's a good way to advertise your passion for painting. One of the most difficult things is the fear of being judged. But seeking and accepting constructive feedback ( 反馈) is crucial to growth. I post my work on social media and, in turn, have met some of the kindest people. They make me feel valued and respected, no matter my level of artistic ability.

The journey you're on won't follow a straight path. You'll hit roadblocks, and you'll feel discouraged at times. Push through, give it time and put in the effort. You will harvest the rewards of an artistic life.
