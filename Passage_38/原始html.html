扫码跟读

I was about thirteen when an uncle gave me a copy of Jostein Gaarder's Sophie's World .It was filled with ideas that were new to me, so I spent the summer with my head in and out of that book.It spoke to me and brought me into a world of philosophy .

That love for philosophy lasted until I got to college.Nothing kills the love for philosophy faster than people who think they understand Foucault, Baudrillard, or Confucius better than you—and then try to explain them.

Eric Weiner's The Socrates Express : In Search of Life Lessons from Dead Philosophers reawakened my love for philosophy.It is not an explanation but an invitation to board an airline flight across centuries, exploring the wisdom of great thinkers.

Weiner's writing is anything but boring .His humorous and narrative style keeps readers engaged.He starts each chapter with a scene on a train ride between cities and then frames each philosopher's work in the context of one thing they can help us do better.His travels take him from the Greek islands to the vibrant streets of India .Throughout his journey, he keeps a journal, similar to a modern-day blog , documenting his thoughts and experiences.The end result is a read in which we learn to wonder like Socrates, see like Thoreau, listen like Schopenhauer, and have no regrets like Nietzsche.This, more than a book about understanding philosophy, is a book about learning to use philosophy to improve our lives.

In the chapter devoted to Confucius, a great thinker who lived around 500 BC , he digs into Confucianism during his visit to China.He focuses on the theme of kindness and proper conduct as pathways to harmony.This resonated ( 引起共鸣 ) with me as I recalled the interactions with my dear aunt and sister , who taught me the meanings of respect and a loving heart.

He makes philosophical thought an appealing exercise that improves the quality of our experiences.Weiner enters into conversation with some of the most important philosophers in history, and he becomes part of that crowd in the process by decoding ( 解读 ) their messages and adding his own interpretation .

The Socrates Express is a fun, sharp book that draws readers in with its apparent simplicity and gradually pulls them in deeper thoughts on desire, loneliness, and aging.The invitation is clear: Weiner wants you to pick up a coffee or tea and sit down with this book.I encourage you to take his offer.It's worth your time, even if time is something we don't have a lot of.

短文译文

我大约 十三 岁的时候，一位 叔叔 给了我一本乔斯坦·贾德的《苏菲的世界》。书中充满了对我而言全新的思想，于是整个夏天我的脑袋一会儿扎进书里，一会儿又从书中抬起（沉浸在书的世界中又偶尔抽离出来）。这本书仿佛与我对话，将我带入了 哲学 的世界。

这份对哲学的热爱一直 持续 到我上大学。没有什么比那些自认为比你更懂福柯、鲍德里亚或 孔子 ，还试图 解释 他们（思想）的人更快地 扼杀 你对哲学的热爱了。

埃里克·韦纳的《苏格拉底 快车 ：从 已故哲学家 那里探寻人生启示》 重新唤起 了我对哲学的热爱。这本书并非是对哲学的 解释 ，而是一张跨越几个世纪的 航 班邀请函，让我们探索伟大思想家的智慧。

韦纳的文笔一点也不 乏味 。他 幽默风趣 、娓娓道来的写作风格能让读者沉浸其中。他以乘坐火车在 城市 间 旅行 的场景作为每章的开头，然后将每位哲学家的思想置于能帮助我们把某件事做得更好的情境中加以 阐述 。他的旅程从希腊 诸岛 一直延伸到 印度 热闹的街道。在整个旅程中，他坚持写日记，就像现在的 博客 一样，记录着他的想法和经历。最终，我们学会了像苏格拉底那样思考，像梭罗那样看世界，像叔本华那样 倾听 ，以及像尼采那样无 怨 无 悔 。这不仅是一本理解哲学的书，更是一本关于学习用哲学改善生活的书。

在专门论述孔子（一位生活在 公元前 大约500年的伟大思想家）的章节中，作者在访问中国期间 深入研究 了 儒家思想 。他专注于仁爱和得体的行为是通往和谐的途径这一主题。这让我产生了共鸣，因为我回想起了我与 亲爱的姨妈 和 姐姐 之间的相处，她们教会了我尊重和爱心的含义。

他将 哲学 思考变成了一种 引人入胜的 活动，能够提升我们体验的质量。韦纳与历史上一些最重要的哲学家展开了 对话 ，在解读他们的 信息 并加入自己的 解释 这个过程中，他也成为这个 群体 的一员。
