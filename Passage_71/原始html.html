Straw weaving ( 草编 ) is an antique method of manufacturing daily items or artworks .It was listed as a national intangible cultural heritage in 2008, for it demonstrates the essence of tradition and the labor of skilled workers.The earliest straw-weaving products were discovered at Hemudu Site, a Neolithic cultural site located in eastern China's Zhejiang Province .Some of these products are now displayed in museums .One can imagine an old fisherman sitting under a woven ( 编织的 ) hat , crafting straw into complex patterns.

One such intangible cultural inheritor , Wu Cui, can turn the straw left over from harvested wheat into eye-catching artworks, such as baskets, hats, and toys for children.Wu explains the process of straw weaving: selection of materials is the first step of a complicated and time-consuming process that can take weeks, or even months, to complete.You need to sketch ( 画素描 ) the piece on paper, soak ( 浸泡 ) the straw and dry it under the sunny skies before beginning to weave.Next comes weaving, shaping and preserving of the work.Some artisans would integrate other fibers like cotton , wool , or gold threads to add texture ( 质地 ) and color to their works.

There are only about 100 individuals involved in the work across the country.Cultural organizations are providing awards to people willing to learn and continue the practice.“The world has changed, and craftsmen need to transcend practicality and pursue the beauty and artistry of straw culture to help the craft survive and thrive ,”Wu says.When asked about her plans in the future, Wu says she will systematically study the local traditional culture and create products to express artistic fantasy .
