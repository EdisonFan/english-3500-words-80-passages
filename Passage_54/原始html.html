扫码跟读

Is comprehension the same whether a person reads a text onscreen or on paper? And are listening to and viewing content as effective as reading the written word when covering the same material? The answers to both questions are often “no”.The reasons relate to a variety of factors, including reduced concentration, an entertainment mindset ( 心态 ), and a tendency to multitask while consuming digital content.

Nowadays, it's common to see an applicant for a job reading notes on a screen while waiting in the department lobby.However, when reading texts of several hundred words or more, learning is generally more successful when it's on paper than onscreen.A large amount of research confirms this finding.The benefits of print reading particularly shine through when experimenters move from posing simple tasks—like identifying the main idea in a reading passage —to ones that require mental abstraction —such as drawing inferences from a text.Reading on paper actually holds significant advantages over reading on screen.

Physical books don't require electricity or batteries.You can read them anywhere, whether you're on the subway , on a plane , or sitting on a park bench.There's no need to worry about battery life or finding a parking spot near a charging station.

Reading on paper allows for better focus.Screens often come with pop-up messages, hyperlinks, and other distractions that can interrupt the flow of reading.In contrast, printed paragraphs help to keep the reader's attention on the material at hand.

The differences between print and digital reading results are partly related to paper's physical properties .With paper, there is a literal laying on of hands, along with the visual geography of distinct pages.Reading under a soft lamp light is easier on the eyes compared to the harsh glare of a screen.People often link their memory of what they've read to where it was on the page.Additionally, the reader can easily add diagrams and notes to the margins of paper, enhancing the personal learning process.

But equally important is the mental aspect.Reading researchers have proposed a theory called the“ shallowing hypothesis ( 假说 )”.According to this theory, people approach digital texts with a mindset suited to social media, which are often not so serious, and devote less mental effort than when they are reading print.

Audio ( 音频 ) and video can feel more engaging than text, and so university teachers increasingly turn to these technologies— say , assigning an online talk instead of an article by the same person.However, psychologists have demonstrated that when adults read news stories, they remember more of the content than if they listen to or view identical pieces.

Digital texts, audio, and video all have educational roles, especially when providing resources not available in print.However, for maximizing learning where mental focus and reflection are called for, educators shouldn't assume all media are the same, even when they contain identical words.

一个人在 屏幕上 阅读文本和在纸上阅读文本时， 理解 程度是一样的吗？涉及相同的材料时，听音频和观看视频是否与阅读书面文字同样有效？这两个问题的答案通常都是否定的。原因与多种因素 相关 ， 包括 注意力下降、娱乐心态以及消费数字内容时倾向于 同时处理好几件事 。

如今，在 部门 大堂里， 求职者 一边等待工作面试，一边阅读屏幕上的笔记，这种情况很常见。然而，在阅读几百字及字数更多的文章时，在纸上阅读 通常 比在屏幕上阅读的学习效果更好。大 量 研究证实了这一结论。当实验者从 设置 简单的任务（如 找出 阅读 文章 的主旨）转向那些需要进行 抽象 思维的任务（如从文本中得出 推论 ）时， 纸质 阅读所带来的益处尤为明显。实际上，与屏幕阅读相比，纸质阅读具有显著的优势。

实体书不需要用 电 ，也不需要电池。你可以在任何地方阅读，无论是在 地铁 里、 飞机 上，还是坐在公园的长椅上。你不必担心电池续航问题，也不必担心要在充电站附近寻找 停车 位。

在纸上阅读能让人更专注。屏幕上经常会出现弹窗消息、超链接和其他干扰因素，这些都会 打断 阅读的连贯性。相比之下，印刷出来的 段落 有助于读者将注意力集中在手头的材料上。

纸质阅读和数字 阅读 在效果上的差异，部分原因与纸张的物理 特性 有关。阅读纸质书时，有 实实在在 用手 触摸 书本的感觉，还有 不同 页面构成的 视觉布局 。与屏幕 刺眼的 强光相比，在柔和的 灯 光下阅读对眼睛更友好。人们常常会将所读内容的记忆与这些内容在书页上的位置联系起来。此外，读者还可以轻松地在书页的空白处添加 图表 和笔记，从而增强个人的学习过程。
