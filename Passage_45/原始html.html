During the rosy years of elementary school ( 小学 ), I enjoyed sharing my dolls and jokes , which allowed me to keep my high social status .I was the queen of the playground .Then came my tweens and teens , and mean girls and cool kids, who had expensive purses and stylish hair .I have noticed this change since October of my ninth school year.The cool kids rose in the ranks not by being friendly but by smoking cigarettes , breaking rules and playing jokes on others, among whom I soon found myself.

Popularity is a well-explored subject in social psychology.Mitch Prinstein, a professor of clinical psychology, sorts the popular into two categories : the likable and the status seekers .The likables' play-well-with-others qualities strengthen schoolyard friendships, jump-start interpersonal skills and, when tapped early, are employed ever after in life and work.They are like a warm April breeze, saying“ hi ”with a friendly smile in the canteen or sharing a hearty laugh with you during a comedy night at the cinema .Then there's the kind of popularity that appears in adolescence : status born of power and even dishonorable behavior like consuming tobacco or alcohol.They may be obsessed with appearances, wearing the latest fashion, perhaps a designer skirt or trousers , and attending every social event just to be seen.Influential figures like the student president or the club chairman are often the topics of their conversations.

Enviable as the cool kids may have seemed, Dr.Prinstein's studies show unpleasant consequences .Those who were highest in status in high school, as well as those least liked in elementary school, are “most likely to engage in dangerous and risky behavior.”

In one study, Dr.Prinstein examined the two types of popularity in 235 adolescents, scoring the least liked, the most liked and the highest in status based on student surveys.“We found that the least liked teens had become more aggressive over time toward their classmates.But so had those who were high in status.It clearly showed that while likability can lead to healthy adjustment, high status has just the opposite effect on us.”We can assume that maintaining a certain image is exhausting, much like carrying around a fifty -pound weight all day long.

Dr.Prinstein has also found that the qualities that made the neighbors want you on a play date —sharing, kindness, openness —carry over to later years and make you better able to relate and connect with others.The likables might join a rugby team for the love of the sport or volunteer at the local zoo out of genuine interest in wildlife.

In analyzing his and other research, Dr.Prinstein came to another conclusion : Not only is likability related to positive life outcomes, but it is also responsible for those outcomes.“Being liked creates opportunities for learning and for new kinds of life experiences that help somebody gain an advantage,”he said.

在小学的 美好 时光里，我喜欢分享我的 玩偶 和 笑话 ，这让我在社交中一直处于很高的 地位 。我成了 操场 上的“ 女王 ”。后来我的 少年过渡期 和 青少年时期 到来了，身边出现了刻薄的女孩和酷酷的孩子，他们拥有昂贵的 包包 ，留着时髦的 发型 。从我上 九 年级的 十月 起，我就注意到了这种变化。酷小孩在群体中的 地位上升 ，靠的不是 友好 ，而是 抽烟 、违反校规和戏弄别人——很快，我就发现自己也是被戏弄的一员。

在社会心理学中，“ 受欢迎程度 ”是一个已被充分探讨过的课题。 临床 心理学教授米奇·普林斯坦将 受欢迎的 人分为两 类 ： 讨人喜爱者 和地位 追求者 。讨人喜爱者善于与人打交道，这种特质能够巩固 校园 友谊， 促进 人际交往技能的发展，如果及早 发掘 ，他们还能将其 运用 到之后的生活和工作中。他们就像 四月 的暖风，在 食堂 友好地笑着和你说“ 嗨 ”，或者在 电影院 的 喜剧 之夜与你一同开怀大笑。而另一种受欢迎的人出现在 青春期 ：其地位 来自 权力，甚至来自 不光彩的 行为，比如抽 烟 、喝酒。他们可能沉迷于外表，穿最新款的时装，也许是名牌 裙子 或 裤子 ，参加各种社交活动，只为让别人看到自己。像学生会 主席 、社团 主席 等具有影响力的人物往往是他们谈论的话题。

尽管酷小孩可能曾看起来 令人羡慕 ，但普林斯坦博士的研究表明，他们的行为会带来令人不快的 后果 。那些在高中时地位最高的人，以及小学时最不受欢迎的人，“最有可能 参与 危险和冒险的行为。”

在一项研究中，普林斯坦博士对235名青少年中的这两类受欢迎者进行了研究。他们根据学生调查，对最不受人喜欢、最受人喜欢和地位最高的青少年进行了评分。“我们发现，随着时间的推移，最不受喜欢的青少年对同学更 有攻击性 。但地位高的青少年也是一样的。这清楚地说明，虽然受人喜欢可以使人健康地适应环境，但较高的地位恰恰会造成 相反的 影响。”我们可以想象，维护某种特定形象是很累人的，就好比整天背着 50 磅的重物四处走一样。
