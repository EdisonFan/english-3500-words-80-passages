# Passage 23

Terri Bolton is a dab hand when it comes to DIY (do-it-yourself). Skilled at doing odd jobs around the house, such as piecing together furniture, fixing pipes and wires, she never pays someone else to do a job she can do herself.

She credits these skills to her late grandfather and builder Derek Lloyd. From the age of six, Terri, now 26, accompanied Derek to work during her school holidays. A day's work was rewarded with £5 in pocket money. She says:" I'm sure I wasn't much of a help to start with, painting the rooms and putting down the flooring throughout the house. It took weeks and it was backbreaking work, but I know he was proud of my skills."

Ms. Bolton, who now rents a house with friends in Wandsworth, South West London, says DIY also saves her from losing any deposit when a tenancy ( 租期) comes to an end. She adds:" I've moved house many times and I always like to personalize my flat and put up pictures, so it's been useful to know how to cover up holes and repaint a room to avoid any charges when I've moved out." With a pair of gloves and sturdy boots, she tackled everything from repairing the roof to installing new plugs and nails, measuring in centimeters to ensure safety.

With millions of people likely to take on DIY projects over the coming weeks, new research shows that more than half of people are planning to make the most of the long, warm summer days to get jobs done. The average spend per project will be around £823. Two thirds of people aim to improve their comfort while at home. Two fifths wish to increase the value of their house. Though DIY has traditionally been seen as a male hobby, the research shows it is women who are now leading the charge. People craft everything from cute scarf hangers to lanterns for the garden.

Alex Gosling, the boss of online estate agency HouseSimple, warns people to recognize their DIY limitations. You might find a leak in the bathroom that almost ruins all your clay tiles ( 瓷砖). Or you might try to hang a vase, only to realize you need better support beams ( 梁)." We're probably all capable of painting walls, but it's a big step to building an extension or taking load-bearing walls down."
