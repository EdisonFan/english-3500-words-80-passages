The memorial to John Todd's environmental legacy lies in his revolutionary eco-machines.When John Todd was a child, he loved to explore the woods around his house, observing how nature solved problems.A dirty stream , for example, often became clear after flowing through plants and along rocks where tiny creatures lived.This is where his journey began as a tale of scientific exploration.When he got older, John started to wonder if this process could be used to clean up the messes people were making.

After studying agriculture , medicine , and fisheries in college, John came to the entrance of the new field of ecological engineering.He went back to observing nature and asking questions.Why can certain plants trap harmful bacteria ( 细菌 )? Which kinds of fish can eat cancer-causing chemicals ? With the right combination of animals and plants, he figured, maybe he could clean up waste the way nature did.He decided to build what he would later call an eco-machine.

The task John set for himself was to remove harmful substances from some sludge ( 污泥 ).First, he constructed a simple format for organizing a series of clear fiberglass ( 玻璃纤维 ) tanks connected to each other.Each tank served as a mini-ecosystem, a vehicle for nature's own purification processes.Then he went around to local ponds and streams and brought back some plants and animals.He placed them in the tanks and waited.Little by little, these different kinds of life got used to one another and formed their own ecosystem .Much like a symphony , these organisms interact in harmony.After a few weeks, John added the sludge.

He was amazed at the results.The plants and animals in the eco-machine took the sludge as food and began to eat it! These living filters work tirelessly to clean the water, requiring minimal human intervention .Within weeks, the sludge had all been digested , and all that was left was pure water .Since then, he has had faith in nature's ability to heal when provided with the right tools.

Over the years John has taken on many big jobs.He developed a greenhouse-like facility that treated sewage ( 污水 ) from 1,600 homes in the South.He also designed a patent eco-machine to clean canal water in Fuzhou, a city in southeast China .

As we face increasing water challenges in the 21st century, John's eco-machines stand as a testament to the healing power of nature, proving that the answers we seek may already be beneath our feet.“ Ecological design” is the name John gives to what he does.“Life on Earth is like a box of spare parts for the inventor ,”he says.“You put organisms in new relationships and observe what happens.”

约翰·托德在环境方面留下功绩的 纪念 体现在他那些具有革命性的生态机器上。在约翰·托德还是个孩子的时候，他就喜欢探索家附近的 树林 ， 观察 大自然是如何解决问题的。例如，一条 污浊的溪流 在 流 经 植物 和 微小生物 栖息的岩石后，往往会变得清澈起来。这就是他科学探险 故事 开始的地方。随着年龄的增长，约翰开始思考这个过程是否可以用来清理人们制造的（环境） 乱象 。

在大学学习了 农业 、 医学 和 渔业 后，约翰进入了生态工程这一新兴领域的 大门 。他重新开始观察自然，并提出问题。为什么某些植物能捕获 有害 细菌？哪种 鱼 能吃掉致癌 化学物质 ？他想，通过 动物 和植物的正确组合，也许他就能像大自然那样净化废物了。他决定制造一种他后来称之为“生态机器”的东西。

约翰为 他自己 设定的任务是从一些污泥中去除有害 物质 。首先，他 构建 了一个简单的 框架 ，用于组织一系列相互连接的透明玻璃纤维 水箱 。每个水箱都充当一个小型生态系统，是大自然自身净化过程的 媒介 。然后，他前往当地的 池塘 和溪流周边， 带回 了一些动植物，把它们放进水箱里，然后等待。渐渐地，这些不同种类的生物开始相互 适应 ，形成了自己的 生态系统 。这些生物就像一部 交响乐 ，和谐地互动着。几周后，约翰把污泥加了进去。
