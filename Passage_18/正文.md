Passage 18

Here's a riddle: What do traffic jams, long lines and waiting for a vacation to start all have in common? There is one answer. They're all situations where we could use a little extra patience.

In the Digital Age, we're used to having what we need immediately and right at our fingertips. However, research suggests that if we practiced patience, we'd be a whole lot better off. Just as a sculpture takes time to shape, only those who wait long enough can reveal the essence of life. Here are several tricks.

Maintain a tidy space

A messy space leads to a confused mind, making it difficult to remain calm during stressful situations. For instance, before rushing through a busy airport, you should organize your wallet and make sure all the important documents such as your cash, passport and ID card are in order.

Take time to contemplate (沉思) and practice gratitude

Setting aside time each day, perhaps during noon or in the evening, to light a candle and focus on your breath. It can also help us foster patience and become thankful for our lives. This practice can be as simple as saying "thank you" to the host when you have a good time, for example. Research shows that thankfulness makes us happier, less stressed and even more optimistic.

Make yourself wait

Instant gratification (满足) may seem like the most "feel good" option at the time, but psychology research suggests waiting for things actually makes us happier in the long run. Start with small tasks. Put off watching your favorite show until the weekend or wait 10 extra minutes before going for that cake. When waiting for a friend who is running late, use the opportunity to read a brochure or take a bus to nearby shops, turning the delay into a productive time. You'll soon find that the more patience you practice, the more you start to apply it to other, more annoying occasions.

Accept the uncomfortable

So many of us have the belief that being comfortable is the only state we will tolerate, and when we experience something outside of our comfort zone, we get impatient about the circumstances. You should learn to say to yourself, "This is merely uncomfortable, not intolerable." You'll then gradually become more patient.
