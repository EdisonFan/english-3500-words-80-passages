Franz Boas's description of Inuit life in the 19th century illustrates the probable moral code of early humans.Here, norms ( 规范 ) were unwritten and rarely expressed clearly, but were well understood and taken to heart. Dishonest and violent behaviors were disapproved of; leadership , marriage, and interactions with other groups were loosely governed by traditions .Conflict was often resolved in musical battles .Because arguing angrily leads to chaos , it was strongly discouraged.With life in the unforgiving northern Canada being so demanding, the Inuit's practical approach to morality made good sense.

The similarity of moral virtues across cultures is striking , even though the relative ranking of the virtues may vary with a social group's history and environment. Typically , cruelty and cheating are discouraged, while cooperation , humbleness, and courage are praised.These universal norms far pre-date the concept of any moralizing religion or written law .Rather, they are rooted in the similarity of basic human needs and our shared mechanisms for learning and problem solving.Our social instincts ( 本能 ) include the intense desire to belong .The approval of others is rewarding, while their disapproval is strongly disliked .These social emotions prepare our brains to shape our behavior according to the norms and values of our family and our community.More generally, social instincts motivate us to learn how to behave in a socially complex world.

The mechanism involves a repurposed reward system originally used to develop habits important for self-care .Our brains use the system to acquire behavioral patterns regarding safe routes home, efficient food gathering, and dangers to avoid.Good habits save time, energy and sometimes your life.Good social habits do something similar in a social context.We learn to tell the truth, even when lying is self-serving ; we help a grandparent even when it is inconvenient .We acquire what we call a sense of right and wrong.

Social benefits are accompanied by social demands: we must get along, but not put up with too much.Hence self-discipline is advantageous .In humans, a greatly enlarged brain boosts self-control , just as it boosts problem-solving skills in the social as well as the physical world.These abilities are strengthened by our capacity for language, which allows social practices to develop in extremely unobvious ways.

弗朗兹·博厄斯对19世纪因纽特人生活的 描述 ，展现了早期人类可能遵循的道德 准则 。在这里（指因纽特社会），规范虽 不成文 ，也很少被清楚地表述出来，但人们都心领神会并铭记于心。 不诚实 和 暴力 行为 不被认可 ； 领导 权、 婚姻 ，以及与其他群体间的互动，都 大致 受到 传统 的 约束 。人们往往通过音乐 较量 化解冲突。因为 愤怒 争吵会引起 混乱 ，所以这种行为遭到 强烈 反对。鉴于加拿大 北部 环境 恶劣 ，生活艰辛，因纽特人这种 务实的道德 观念可谓合情合理。

不同文化中，道德 美德 有着 惊人的相似性 ，尽管因为社会群体的历史和环境各异，美德相对的重要性在排序上可能有所差异。 通常来说 ，残酷和 欺骗 不受鼓励，而 合作 、 谦逊 和勇气会受到赞扬。这些 普遍的 规范远远 早于 任何 道德化的 宗教或成文 法规 而存在。更确切地说，它们植根于人类基本需求的相似性，以及人类共有的学习和解决问题的机制。我们的社会本能包括强烈的 归属 欲。他人的 赞同 会带来愉悦感，而他人的 反对 则令人 厌恶 至极。这些社会情感使我们的大脑 做好准备 ，从而根据家庭和社群的规范和价值观来塑造自身行为。更普遍地说，社会本能促使我们学习如何在复杂的 社会 环境中 行事 。
