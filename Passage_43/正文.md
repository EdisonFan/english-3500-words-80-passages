# Passage 43

Friendship needs care and attention to keep it in good health. Here are five ways to sustain long-distance friendships.

Set a regular date.

Long-lasting friendships share the characteristic that both sides equally contact and share with one another. With busy schedules, squeezing in phone calls can be a challenge. Try to find a time that works for both of you and stick to your regular appointments.

More isn't always merrier.

Make sure you have communicated with your friend about how frequently each of you wants to be contacted and which means of contact works best for you both. Compensate by writing letters. There are alternatives to consistent written communication, including leaving voice messages or having a group chat.

Practise empathy ( 共情).

You may be the friend who left or the one who was left behind. The friend who stays needs to be sensitive to all the additional time demands of the friend who has moved, for example, to Germany for a scholarship or to Australia for work. The one in the new environment should be sympathetic to the fact that your friend may feel abandoned. In a word, the key is to remain sincerely invested in each other's lives.

Remember important dates.

Anniversaries and birthdays carry even more weight in long-distance friendships. Although various software, apps and Wi-Fi connection might make day-to-day communication possible, extra effort goes a long way on special days. Simply keeping a diary that keeps track of friends' birthdays and other important dates will make sure nothing slips by you. Send thoughtful postcards during Christmas or other celebrations—these tangible reminders of your friendship carry more weight than digital messages alone.

Don't rely on technology alone.

Thanks to digital communications, distance no longer means complete separation. However, long-distance friendships—even close ones—require more conscious effort to thrive across time zones and continents. Try to seek out chances to renew friendships. How to do it? Just spend face-to-face time together at a café or bakery whenever possible.
