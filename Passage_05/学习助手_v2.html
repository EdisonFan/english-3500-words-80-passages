01

Our teacher, Miss Chevalier, was a small woman with a moon face, fattyfingers and curlsthat sprang straight up from her head, hencethe funny name “Poodle ( <b> 卷毛狗 </b> )”.She taught in our reading club.Sometimes she would look in and ask what we were reading—not to check but to know.

02

That's what happened the day my club was reading aloud a poemby Henry Longfellow. I guess I was better than the others, for Miss Chevalier asked a while later,“Addie, I was wonderingif you would recitethe whole poem to the Saturday evening's club.”She said a famousprofessor was going to give a lecture about the poet, and a presentationof that poem would be a fresh way to start.

03

She asked me to memorizeit.“But that shouldn't be a problem for a childof your ability,”she added.I'm telling you, my happy feet barely touched the groundall the way home.To me, that was the biggest thing ever and I soon learnedthe whole poem by heart, well prepared for our first rehearsal.

04

I was desperatelynervous when I went to her officethe nextday.But the good thing was she always had the smile that would make you feel like you just did something right.

05

Halfwaythrough the poem, Miss Chevalier stoppedme and asked if I knew what “impetuous” meant. Despiteher encouragingsmile, I wanted to sinkthrough the floorbecause I was unsureabout its pronunciationas well as its meaning.Miss Chevalier pretendednot to have noticedmy redface and handedme a dictionary, asking me to read its definition aloud .“Impetuous has two definitions: rushingwith great forceor violence; acting suddenly, with littlethought.”

06

She asked me which one fitted the poem.I read bothdefinitions over again, trying to figure it out, but Miss Chevalier must have read my mind.“There is no wronganswer,”she said,“I want to know your opinion, Addie.” Hesitantly, I said,“ Maybe...both.”

07

She liked that.“One has to be impetuous both ways or they wouldn't dareto face up to any challenge.Would you call yourselfimpetuous?”

08

I knew she was asking for an opinion.“My motherthinks being impetuous for girls is improper, anyway.”

09

She said my mom was somewhatright about that.“But girls should also be wiseto take up challenges when needed.I believeyou are such a girl.”

10

After that, I would nevercall Miss Chevalier “Poodle” again.

PARAGRAPH 01

我们的老师谢瓦利埃小姐是个身材矮 小的女人 ，长着一张圆脸， 胖乎乎的 手指，还有从头顶 直接 向上 翘 起的 卷发 ， 因此 得了“卷毛狗”这个 有趣的绰号 。她在我们读书 俱乐部 任教。有时她会进来看看，问问我们正在读什么——不是为了检查，而是为了了解。

PARAGRAPH 02

那是我们俱乐部正在大声朗读亨利·朗费罗的一首 诗 的那 天 发生的事。我猜想我读得比别人好，因为 过了 一会儿，谢瓦利埃老师问我：“艾迪，我在 想 你是否愿意为周六晚上的俱乐部 朗诵 整首诗。”她说有一位 知名 教授要做关于这位 诗人 的演讲，而 展示 这首诗将是一个新颖的开场方式。

PARAGRAPH 03

她让我把诗 背下来 ，并且补充道：“不过，对于像你这样有能力的 孩子 来说，这应该不成问题。”说真的，回家的路上，我高兴得脚 几乎没沾 过 地 。对我来说，这是有史以来最重要的事，我很快就把整首诗熟 记 于 心 了，为我们的第一次 排练 做好了充分准备。

PARAGRAPH 04

第二 天去她 办公室 的时候，我紧张得 要命 。但 好在 她总是面带微笑，让你觉得自己刚刚做了一件正确的事。

PARAGRAPH 05

诗背到 一半 时，谢瓦利埃小姐 打断 了我，问我是否知道“impetuous”这个词的意思。 尽管 她带着 鼓励的 微笑，但我还是想找个 地 缝 钻 进去，因为我 不确定 这个词的 发音 ，也不知道它的 意思 。谢瓦利埃小姐 假装 没有 注意到 我涨 红 的脸， 递给 我一本词典，让我 大声 读出它的 释义 。“impetuous有两个释义：以极大的 力量 或 暴力猛冲 ；不假思索地突然行动。”

PARAGRAPH 06

她问我哪个释义适合这首诗。我把 两个 释义又 都 读了一遍，试图弄清楚，但谢瓦利埃小姐一定是看穿了我的 心思 ，她说：“没有 错误的 答案，我只想知道你的看法，艾迪。”我 犹豫了一下 ，说：“ 也许 ……两个都适合。”

PARAGRAPH 07

她喜欢这个回答。“一个人必须有这两种冲动，否则就不会 敢于 面对任何 挑战 。你觉得 你自己 冲动吗？”

PARAGRAPH 08

我知道她想听听我的看法。“ 不管怎样 ，我 妈妈 觉得女孩冲动是 不妥当的 。”

PARAGRAPH 09

她说我妈妈说得 有几分 道理。“但女孩也应该在需要时 明智 地接受挑战。我 相信 你就是这样的女孩。”
