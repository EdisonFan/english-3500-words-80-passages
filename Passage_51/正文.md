# Passage 51

Travel promotes understanding, expands our minds, and makes us better people. But the rapid growth of travel has led to overtourism in certain regions and destinations. From ancient temples to areas along the coast, popular destinations are struggling with the virtual circus of crowds. While local economies and communities benefit from tourist spending in hotels, restaurants, and shops, the negative impacts on civilian life and cultural heritage are becoming apparent. Many cities are now implementing measures to prohibit certain tourist activities and ban new tourist shops around historic sites. So, should we stop traveling? Certainly not. The loss of what travel offers would be unacceptable. Here are some tips on making wise decisions to minimize pressure on the places we visit and improve our experience.

Choose mindfully. Over-visited destinations are that way for a reason: they're special. With so many online posts featuring the same places, it's easy to feel like you're missing out. Go somewhere only when the landscape, culture, or food deeply draw you. Travel for yourself and no one else.

Get creative. The best way to ease pressure on over-touristed destinations is to go somewhere else. Though overtourism is described as a problem affecting the entire world, it's actually concentrated in a small number of extremely popular spots. That means you have tons of less-visited options instead of joining the crowd. You can still find relatively undiscovered places. It's advisable to try a regional alternative or seek out local cuisine in residential districts. Reserving a room in a local cottage rather than a chain hotel also offers better cultural immersion.

Schedule your travel beyond the typical tourist circuit. Maximize your experience by skipping major holidays or peak seasons like July or August. Wisely plan your departure time by choosing early morning or evening hours, which might be slightly less ideal. You'll compete with fewer tourists, save money, experience a different side of a popular place, and boost the economy during traditionally slower tourism periods.

Visiting a place that others call home is a privilege. Do your part to preserve what makes a destination special in the first place. Consider giving back to the communities you're visiting. This might mean higher fees for attractions, but your effort will benefit both other visitors and local communities in the long run.
