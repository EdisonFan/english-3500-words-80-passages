Popularization has in some cases changed the original meaning of emotional intelligence.Many people now misunderstand emotional intelligence as almost everything desirable in a person's makeup that cannot be measured by an IQ test, such as character, motivation, confidence, mental stability , optimism and “people skills.”People tend to interpret, for example, being shy as a representation of low EQ.

In fact, EQ is about recognizing, understanding, and managing emotions—both one's own and those of others.When an employee is preparing for an important presentation, he might feel embarrassed to admit his anxiety, so he dresses in his best suit, complete with a sharp jacket .But he sweats a lot and constantly adjusts his belt .A manager with high emotional intelligence would notice these cues and offer support for the public presentation.

Emotional intelligence is a specific set of skills that can be used for either good or bad purposes.The ability to accurately understand how others are feeling may be used by a doctor to find out how best to help her patients, while a cheater might use it to control potential victims .Being emotionally intelligent does not necessarily make one a moral person.

Popular beliefs regarding emotional intelligence run far ahead of what research can reasonably support, and even serve as a modern justice for human development.Some might protest its widespread adoption, but despite some mostly understood aspects and prejudices surrounding the concept, the overall effects of the publicity have been more beneficial than harmful.The most positive aspect of this popularization is a new and much needed emphasis on mercy and empathy by employers , educators and others interested in promoting social well-being .For example, instead of focusing solely on academic achievements, schools now restore balance by prioritizing emotional learning.The popularization has helped both the public and researchers re-evaluate the functionality of emotions and how they serve people adaptively in everyday life.

Although the continuing popular appeal of emotional intelligence is desirable, we hope that such attention will excite a reform in the scientific and scholarly study of emotion.It is our hope that in the coming decades , advances in science will provide a worthy framework for understanding and managing emotions.Emotional intelligence, with its focus on both head and heart, may serve to point us in the right direction.
