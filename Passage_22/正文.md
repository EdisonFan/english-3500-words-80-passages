# Passage 22

Writing an essay is a difficult process for most people.However, the process can be made easier if you learn to practice three simple techniques, which will be just the right tools in your kit .

First of all, learn the technique of nonstop writing.When you are first trying to think of ideas for an essay, put your pen to your paper and write nonstop for ten or fifteen minutes without letting your pen leave the paper.Picture a spy in the depths of your mind, capturing every thought before it vanishes .Stay loose and free to get your fluent thoughts in motion.Let your pen follow the waves of thought before self-doubt can drag you down.The goal is to keep your literary muscles moving.Your pen will become your weapon against procrastination ( 拖延 ).Even though this technique won't work for everyone, it helps many people get a good store of ideas to draw on.

The next technique is to write your draft rapidly without worrying about being perfect.Too many writers try to polish their drafts to get them right the first time.Yet, by learning to live with imperfection, you will save yourself headaches and a wastepaper basket full of crumpled ( 弄皱 ) paper.Think of your first draft as a path cut out of the jungle with a knife while it is still sharp—as part of an exploration , not as a complete highway without any friction .

The third technique is to try printing out a triple-spaced ( 三倍行距 ) copy to allow space for revision .Many beginning writers don't leave enough space to revise .As a result, these writers never get in the habit of crossing out chunks ( 大块 ) of their draft and writing revisions in the blank space.With a physical copy, you can use an eraser , glue , and tape to perfect your work like a tailor crafting a suit.After you have revised your draft until it is too messy to work from anymore , you can enter your changes into your word processor .Then you can print out a fresh draft, again setting your text on triple-space.The resulting blank space invites you to weigh each word carefully.
