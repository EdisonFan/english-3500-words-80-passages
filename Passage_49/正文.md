# Passage 49

Much of the work in today's world is accomplished ( 完成) in teams. Most people believe the best way to build a great team is to gather a group of the most talented individuals. Sports team owners spend millions of dollars to trade for top talent in the hope of qualifying for championships. However, the salary cap often becomes a significant constraint, forcing teams to make difficult decisions. Companies spend millions hiring top business people. Is their money well spent?

Several recent studies examined the role of talent in the sports world. They focused on football, basketball and baseball. The results are mixed. For football and basketball, adding talented players to a team proves a good method, but only up to the point where 70% of the players are top talent; above that level, the team's performance begins to decline. Interestingly, this trend isn't evident in baseball, where additional individual talent keeps improving the team's performance.

To explain this phenomenon, the researchers explored the degree to which a good performance by a team requires its members to coordinate ( 协调) their actions. This task interdependence distinguishes baseball from football and basketball. In baseball, the performance of individual players is less dependent on teammates. They conclude that a sports team is like a ship navigating through tough games, when the chemistry between players becomes critical, the ship's voyage will be troubled if there is too much talent aboard. If a basketball star, for example, thinks that he deserves a high personal point total during a season, he may take a shot himself when it would be better to pass the ball to a teammate. Young children learning to play team sports are often told,“There is no I in TEAM.” Stars apparently do not follow this basic principle of sportsmanship.

Another possibility is that when there is a lot of talent in league matches, some players may make less effort. Just as in a game of tug-of-war ( 拔河比赛), whenever a person is added, everyone else pulls the rope with less force. They might feel less responsible when wearing their team uniform. Since only collective effort is visible, the individual contribution of each person becomes less so.

Moreover, the commercial aspects of running a team require careful balance. While star players may boost ticket sales by satisfying their fans, their costly contracts can use up resources needed for other essential areas.

Building up a dream team is complex. Simply hiring the best talent may not be the right recipe. An A-team may require a balance—not just A players, but a few generous B players as well.
