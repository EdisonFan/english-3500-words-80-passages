Sometimes scientists are accused of conducting too many human-centered studies, and we feel guilty about not knowing how smart animals actually are.Recently, a team of researchers trained three Rhesus monkeys （恒河猴） to associate 26 clearly different symbols consisting of numbers and selective letters with 0-25 drops of water or juice as a bonus .The training procedure was thorough , as it ensured the monkeys understood that each symbol represented a specific quantity.The researchers then tested how the monkeys combined —or added—the symbols to get the reward. Anyhow , the researchers intended to clarify whether the monkeys could understand the concept of addition.

Here's how Harvard Medical School scientist Margaret Livingstone, who led the team, described the experiment: In their cages, the monkeys were provided with touch screens .On one part of the screen, a symbol would appear, and on the other side two symbols inside a circle were shown.For example, the number 7 would flash on one side of the screen and the other end would have 9 and 8.If the monkeys reached out their arms to touch the left side of the screen, they would be rewarded with seven drops of water or juice; if they went for the circle, they would be rewarded with the sum of the numbers— seventeen in this example.

After running hundreds of tests, the researchers noted that the monkeys would go for the higher values more than half the time.While the monkeys were not addicts of maths , they were performing a calculation, not just memorizing the value of each combination.

When the team examined the results of the experiment more closely , they noticed that the monkeys tended to underestimate a sum compared with a single symbol when the two were close in value.Sometimes, for example, they estimated 13 to be more rewarding than the sum of 8 and 6.The underestimation was systematic : When adding two numbers, the monkeys always paid attention to the larger of the two, and then added only a fraction （小部分） of the smaller number to it.

According to Dr.Livingstone's brief conclusion, this indicates that there is “a certain way quantity is represented in their brains”.This experiment extended our understanding of animal cognition, opening up new avenues in the discipline of comparative psychology.

短文译文

有时，科学家们会因开展过多以人类为中心的研究而受到 指责 ，我们也会因不了解动物实际上有多聪明而感到 愧疚 。最近，一个研究小组训练了三只恒河猴，让它们将26个明显不同的符号与0到25 滴 作为 奖励 的水或 果汁 联系起来，这些符号是由数字和严格挑选的字母组成的。训练 过程 十分 全面 ，因为它确保了这些恒河猴明白每个符号都代表着特定的数量。然后，研究人员测试了这些猴子是如何 组合 ——或者相加——这些符号来获得奖励的。 不管怎样 ，研究人员 旨在弄清楚 这些猴子能否理解加法的概念。

以下是带领这个研究团队的哈佛 医学 院 科学家 玛格丽特·利文斯通对该实验的描述：在猴子们的笼子里配备了触摸 屏 。在屏幕的一侧会出现一个符号，而另一侧则会在一个 圆圈 中显示两个符号。例如，数字7会在屏幕的一侧 闪烁 ，另一侧则显示9和8。如果猴子伸出 手臂 触摸屏幕左侧，就会被奖励 7 滴水或果汁；如果选择圆圈，它们得到的奖励数量就相当于两个数字的总和——在这个例子中是 17 。

在进行了数百次测试后，研究人员 注意到 ，猴子们选择更大 数值 的次数超过了一半。虽然这些猴子并不是 数学迷 ，但它们确实在进行计算，而不仅仅是记住每个组合的数值。
