Passage 13

Habit formation is the process by which behaviors become automatic. People develop countless habits as they explore the world, whether they are aware of them or not. Understanding how habits take shape may be helpful in changing bad habits.

Habits are built through learning and repetition. A person is thought to develop a habit in the course of pursuing goals by beginning to associate certain environmental cues (暗示) with behavioral responses that help meet the goal. For example, you might associate the touch of the kettle button, or the taste of ham on toast to the start of a new day. Over time, thoughts of the behavior and ultimately the behavior itself are likely to be triggered (触发) by these cues. Therefore, if you want to make running part of your routine, try placing your running socks and shoes next to your bed as a visual trigger.

A "habit loop (环)" is a way of describing several related elements that produce habits. These elements are called the cue, the routine, and the reward. For example, stress could serve as a cue that one responds to by eating, which produces the reward—the reduction of stress. While a routine involves repeated behavior, it is not necessarily performed in response to a deep-rooted urge, much like a postman following the same route daily without conscious thought.

Old habits can be difficult to shake, and healthy habits are often harder to develop. But through repetition, it is possible to form new habits. The length of time needed to build a habit will depend on multiple factors, including the individual and the intended behavior. While a bad habit can be formed in a few weeks, it takes many months, approximately ninety days, to build a healthy habit. Take some time to think about what leads to bad habits and re-evaluate what you get out of them (or do not). Consider and keep in mind why you want to make a change, including how the change reflects your values and blesses your life in meaningful ways.
