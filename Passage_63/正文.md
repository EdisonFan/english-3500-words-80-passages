# Passage 63

Let's take a minute to think about how precious water is. It forms an important part of the earth's atmosphere, and the human body is 60% water. When we are thirsty, we will fill a glass with clean water. Without water, even simple tasks like using the washroom or preparing meals would become challenging. Furthermore, water is essential to not only humans, but also to other species such as dolphins and frogs that rely on clean water for survival.

We use water indirectly too. For example, farmers use water to make the plants grow. Without sufficient water, plants may dry out. Many factories also use water during the production of clothing, textiles ( 纺织品) and so on.

The truth is that we are lucky enough to have clean water whenever we want, but this is not the case for many people around the world. The lack of water can lead to severe crises such as drought. This is a significant concern worldwide, especially in Africa. Rivers and lakes are drying up, causing a decrease in water supply for both drinking and farming.

Pollution is another major problem that causes the shortage of clean water. Waste materials dumped ( 倾倒) into rivers and the ocean pose a threat to both the environment and public health. If we drink dirty water, we can catch diseases and become ill. Every year over 500,000 children die from diarrhea ( 腹泻) caused by dirty water. Also, in some countries, children walk many kilometers every day to get water. If children spend hours each day collecting water, they can't go to school for an education.

Economic support and proper planning can help supply safe water to those in need. In 1993 the United Nations decided that March 22nd would be World Water Day. During this annual event, countries around the world hold activities to educate people about water issues. It provides an opportunity to advocate for innovative solutions for water management. Schools organize campaigns to promote water conservation. They make posters and hold competitions to encourage inventions for solving water shortages. Some students take trips to the local beach to collect rubbish and clean up the sea. At one school in the UK, children walk 6 km with 6 liters of water. In this way, they know how it feels to walk a long distance carrying heavy bottles.
