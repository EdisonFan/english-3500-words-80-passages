扫码跟读

Research sometimes proves, with data , what we more or less already know ( e.g. Exercise is good for you, and polluted air isn't).Still, sometimes our assumptions are incorrect , and scientific findings surprise researchers.That's because we have no access to first-hand information unless we actually wander through forests and valleys .A recent example is the phenomenon of disappearing lakes in parts of the frozen treeless Arctic region.

You might think these lakes would be expanding, not disappearing.As climate change warms the region—melting ( 融化 ) surface snow and ice as well as the permafrost ( 永冻层 )—there should be more surface water, forming larger lakes and even new ones.Recently, however, scientists have observed not just shrinking ( 缩小的 ) lakes but a mass disappearance of lakes.

Many scientists shared the commonsense expectation that as the underground permafrost melted, lakes would at first expand with meltwater ( 融水 ) flowing into them.Eventually, researchers made a prediction that progressive warming during the 21st and 22nd centuries would dry out the Arctic, and lakes would begin to shrink.But now it looks as if the polar waters are disappearing a century sooner than predicted.A case in point is that some large, age-old lakes shrank dramatically in a matter of months.Scientists have labeled this phenomenon“ disastrous lake loss”.

The permafrost is very solid, but in reality, there are spaces between the rocks, minerals , and organic matter that make it up.And because these spaces are filled with solid ice, like natural time capsules , liquid water cannot readily get inside, but it can when the permafrost collapses under the combined effects of warming and other factors, causing the water to pour away, leaving behind nothing but dry basins .Moreover, with the liberation of this ancient water, the trapped carbon is released , leading to further warming.

Why did scientists miss this? One probable reason is that most climate models assume that the melting of permafrost is driven only by warming air.However, new evidence suggests that rainfall —particularly increasing autumn rain—is now contributing significantly to permafrost loss.The rainfall carries heat into the ground.Yet none of the existing models includes such processes.

That is a good example of why—no matter how accurate our statistics are or will one day be—we need direct observation of the natural world.A detective must first go to the crime scene before piecing together evidence.Similarly, scientists should actively observe their subject, whether it's the smallest cell under a microscope or vast formations in a cave .

研究有时会用 数据 证明我们或多或少已经知道的事情（ 例如 ，锻炼对你有好处，而 污染 的空气则不然）。不过，有时我们的 假设 并 不正确 ，科学研究发现会让研究人员感到惊讶。这是因为， 除非 我们真的 漫步 于森林和 山谷 之中，否则我们无法获得第一手信息。最近的一个例子是，在北极地区一些终年 冰封 且 无树木生长的 区域湖泊逐渐 消失 的现象。

你可能认为这些湖泊会扩大，而不是消失。随着气候变化使这片区域变暖——融化了 地表积雪 和冰层以及永冻层——地表水应该会变多，从而形成更大的湖泊，乃至新的湖泊。然而，最近科学家们不仅观察到湖泊缩小的情况，还发现湖泊在 大规模 消失。

许多科学家都怀有一种常识性的预期，即随着 地下 永冻层的融化，湖泊首先会因融水的流入而扩大。最终，研究人员做出了一个预测，即在21世纪和22世纪， 逐渐 变暖的气候会使北极地区干涸，而湖泊也将会开始缩小。然而，现在看来， 极地 水域消失的时间似乎比原先预测的提前了一个世纪。一个典型的例子是，一些 古老的 大型湖泊在短短几个月内急剧缩小。科学家们 称 这一现象为“ 灾难性的 湖泊流失”。

永冻层非常坚固，但实际上，构成永冻层的岩石、 矿物质 和有机物之间存在着空隙。这些空隙被像天然时间 胶囊 一样坚固的冰填满， 液态 水无法 轻易地 渗入其中。但当永冻层在气候变暖和其他因素的共同作用下 崩塌 时，液态水就可以进入，使得水倾 泻 而下，留下的只有干涸的 盆地 。此外，随着这些古老水源被 释放 出来，被困的碳也随之 释放 ，导致气候进一步变暖。
